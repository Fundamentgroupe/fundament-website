Research allowance: the plannable building block

The research allowance is a tax incentive under the German Research Allowance Act (FZulG). It is open to companies of any size and sector, has no call for proposals and can be claimed retroactively. For small and medium-sized enterprises the rate is 35%, for larger companies 25% of the eligible expenses. Details are on our page research allowance in Germany.

Project funding: grants for specific projects

When a development project is only starting, grant programmes from the federal government, the federal states and the EU come into question. They are mostly tied to selection procedures and deadlines and are usually applied for before the project starts. Please note: the Central Innovation Programme for SMEs (ZIM) has had a temporary application freeze since July 2026. A restart is aimed for in 2027 but has not been promised. Companies that are already developing can use the research allowance in the meantime. We check which programme fits your project, prepare the application documents and check what can be combined with the research allowance.

Other programmes: energy, digitalisation, investment

Besides research and development, there is funding for energy and resource efficiency, for digitalisation and for investment, partly as grants and partly as low-interest subsidised loans. Programmes, terms and application windows change all the time. That is why we clarify the current status in a conversation, not on a static page.

Combining programmes without applying twice

Funding programmes follow different rules and deadlines. Funding cannot be added up without limit for the same costs, because cumulation rules apply. We therefore look at all programmes that come into question together and decide which costs belong in which programme.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers.

Which grants are available for research and development in Germany?

The main building blocks are the research allowance, a tax incentive with a legal entitlement, project funding from the federal government, the federal states and the EU, and further programmes for energy efficiency, digitalisation and investment. Which of them fit your project depends on the type and stage of the project.

When do I have to apply for funding?

That depends on the programme. For project funding you usually have to apply before the project starts. The research allowance, by contrast, can also be claimed retroactively for past years, up to four years after the end of the calendar year in which the expenses were incurred. So it pays to look at planned projects early.

Can several funding programmes be combined?

In part. Funding cannot be added up without limit for the same costs, because cumulation rules apply. We check in advance how programmes can sensibly be split, so that nothing is applied for twice and nothing is left unused.
