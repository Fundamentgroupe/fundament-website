Research allowance consulting on a success basis: fee models and what to check

Many advisers for the research allowance work on a success basis. The models differ a lot. Here is what is behind them and which questions to ask before you hire anyone.

What does consulting on a success basis mean?

The fee depends on whether, and in what amount, the research allowance is granted. If no allowance is granted, there is usually no fee. Upfront payments or a base fee are not usual in pure success models.

Which fee models exist?

  • Success fee: a share of the allowance that is granted. The risk lies mostly with the adviser, and the amount depends on the result.
  • Fixed price: an amount agreed in advance per project or application year. It is predictable, and the risk lies with the company.
  • Time-based fee: billed by the hour or by day rate. The effort can be hard to predict.
  • Mixed models: a base fee plus a success share. Look closely at both parts.

What to check in a success fee

  • Reference amount: Is the share based on the allowance applied for or the allowance actually granted?
  • Due date: Is the fee due after approval, or only when the allowance is paid out or offset?
  • Partial rejection: Does the fee fall if less is granted than applied for?
  • Tiers and later years: Is the rate the same for all years, and how are high amounts calculated?
  • Term and termination: Are there minimum terms, exclusivity or automatic renewal?
  • Scope of work: Who writes the project descriptions and records, and who handles follow-up questions and objections?
  • Data protection and confidentiality: Is there a non-disclosure agreement and, if needed, a data processing agreement?
  • Role of the tax advisor: The company is entitled to apply; submission to the tax office is usually done through the tax advisor.

Ten questions to ask any adviser

  1. How high is the fee, and what exactly does it refer to?
  2. When is it due?
  3. What happens in case of partial rejection or rejection?
  4. Is there an upfront payment, a base fee or a minimum turnover?
  5. How long does the contract bind me, and how can I terminate it?
  6. Who prepares the documents, and who is my contact?
  7. How is eligibility assessed in advance, and will you say honestly if it does not fit?
  8. How do you work with my tax advisor?
  9. How do you handle my development data?
  10. Do I receive the terms in writing before I sign?

Frequently asked questions

Short answers.

What does research allowance consulting cost?

It depends on the model. With success fees, the amount follows the granted allowance; with fixed prices, the agreed amount; with time-based fees, the time spent. What matters is that the terms are in writing before you hire anyone.

Is consulting on a success basis always cheaper?

Not automatically. A success fee shares the risk differently, but it can cost more than a fixed price when the allowance is high. Compare the reference amount, tiers and due date, not just the percentage.

When does the fee become due at Fundament?

Only after approval. There is no upfront payment. If no funding is granted, no fee arises, not even a partial one.

Who is responsible for the application?

The company is entitled to apply. Advisers prepare the technical content, and submission to the tax office usually runs through the tax advisor or the company's own access.
