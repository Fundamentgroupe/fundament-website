German research allowance (Forschungszulage): eligibility, process and amount

The research allowance is a tax incentive for research and development under the German Research Allowance Act (FZulG). This page explains who is eligible, what is funded, how high the allowance is and how the application works.

Who is eligible?

Any company that is liable to tax in Germany can claim the allowance, regardless of size, legal form or sector. This applies to a mid-sized manufacturer as much as to a technology company. What matters is not who you are, but whether a project meets the criteria for research and development.

What counts as research and development?

The key test is technical uncertainty: at the start of a project, it was open whether and how the goal could be reached. You do not need a laboratory. Typical examples:

  • a new machine or plant control system with uncertain behaviour under load
  • a material test or a new manufacturing process
  • a new formulation, or scaling up from the lab to production
  • a software architecture, an algorithm or an AI model with an open outcome

Not eligible are the plain application of known methods, design to standards, customer adaptation without technical uncertainty, and ongoing quality assurance. Drawing this line is the most demanding part of every application.

Eligible costs include in particular:

  • personnel costs of employees working on development projects (pro rata by working time)
  • own work of sole proprietors and partners in a partnership: EUR 100 per hour, up to 40 hours per week
  • contract research: 70% of the fee can be counted
  • a flat rate of 20% for overheads on eligible costs, without individual proof, for projects started after 31 December 2025

Example (SME, one application year): EUR 320,000 personnel costs plus the 20% overhead flat rate (EUR 64,000) give a cost base of EUR 384,000. At 35%, the allowance is EUR 134,400. This is a rough estimate only. The actual amount depends on how projects are delineated and on your cost structure.

How does the application work?

  1. Define and describe the projects. Which projects meet the criteria, and what is the technical reasoning?
  2. Application to the certification body (Bescheinigungsstelle Forschungszulage). It checks whether the project is research and development. The application is free of charge, and processing usually takes about three months.
  3. Application for the allowance at the tax office. With the certificate, the allowance is determined and then credited against the tax liability or paid out.

By law, only the company itself can apply, so the application is submitted through your own access or your tax adviser. Fundament prepares the application in full and supports you with queries from the certification body and the tax office.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers.

What is the research allowance?

The research allowance (Forschungszulage) is a tax incentive for research and development under the German Research Allowance Act (FZulG). Companies receive a share of their eligible costs as an allowance: 35% for small and medium-sized enterprises, 25% for larger ones. The allowance is credited against the tax liability or paid out.

Who can apply?

Any company that is liable to tax in Germany, regardless of size, legal form or sector. What matters is not who you are, but whether your project meets the criteria for research and development. By law, only the company itself, or an authorised person using the company's own electronic access, can apply.

Can we apply for past years?

In general, yes. Applications can be made retroactively, up to four years after the end of the calendar year in which the costs were incurred. In practice this often gives the largest volume, because several years can be claimed at the same time.
